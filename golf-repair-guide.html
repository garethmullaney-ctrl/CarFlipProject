1 · Paint code — solve colour before you buy

The golden rule for a first flip: buy panels already in the car's exact colour rather than respraying — until you've practised paint. A wing or mirror bought in the right code bolts straight on with, at most, a light blend; the wrong colour means a full respray, which is where DIY jobs start to look like salvage.

Where the code is

  • Spare-wheel well — lift the boot floor; there's usually a sticker with the paint code (most reliable spot).
  • Driver's door shut / B-pillar — the vehicle data sticker.
  • Service book or the original dealer invoice, if present.

What you're likely looking at (black Golf Mk6)

CodeColourHow to tell
LC9Z / A1Black Uni (solid)Flat black, no sparkle in sunlight. Common on base S trim — most likely on this car. Easiest to match & blend.
LC9X / 2TDeep Black Pearl (metallic)Fine sparkle/flake in direct light. Needs a proper blend into the door to hide — hand to a bodyshop if so.

Confirm the code on the car, then buy the wing and mirror in that exact code. If it's solid LC9Z, a first-timer can realistically DIY the lot. If it's metallic LC9X, still buy colour-matched panels but budget a bodyshop blend.

4 · Job 1 — Nearside front wing swap BEGINNER-FRIENDLY · bolts on

This is the ideal first bodywork job because the Golf wing is bolted on, not welded — you're undoing ~6–8 bolts and doing them back up. Allow 1.5–3 hours the first time.

1 Jack the front nearside, remove the road wheel (axle stand under the sill — never rely on the jack).
2 Peel back the wheel-arch liner — a few Torx screws and push-clips with the trim tool. This exposes the lower and rear wing bolts.
3 Open the bonnet — the top row of wing bolts runs along the top edge under the bonnet shut line (usually 3–4).
4 Open the front door — one or two bolts sit at the rear edge of the wing by the A-pillar/door hinge, plus one at the bottom by the sill.
5 Free the front edge — the front of the wing tucks behind the bumper corner; ease the bumper edge out (a couple of clips) rather than forcing it. Lift the old wing away.
6 Offer up the new wing finger-tight — start every bolt but leave them loose. Now set the panel gaps: even to the bonnet, the door, and the bumper. Patience here is the whole job — a wing that lines up looks factory; a rushed one shouts "repaired."
7 Nip the bolts up in stages once the gaps are even, refit the arch liner and wheel, drop it off the stand.

5 · Job 2 — Driver's door mirror swap BEGINNER-FRIENDLY

Allow 20–40 minutes.

1 Prise off the inner triangular trim at the base of the mirror (door top corner) with the trim tool — it clips off.
2 Unplug the mirror wiring connector (heated/electric mirror) behind the trim.
3 Undo the 3 bolts (usually Torx) holding the mirror to the door and lift the old unit off.
4 Fit the colour-matched replacement — bolt on, reconnect the plug, refit the trim. Test the glass adjusts and heats.

7 · Order of operations

  • Day 0: pay Copart, book collection, file the V62.
  • On arrival: find the paint code → order the colour-matched wing + mirror.
  • Cold-start check the engine (listen for chain rattle) before spending on cosmetics — it's the one thing that could change the plan.
  • While parts ship: sort emissions (service + good run), check the NSF wheel/hub.
  • Parts in: wing first, then mirror, then blend/paint if needed.
  • Finish: full valet, get it MOT-clean on emissions, photograph, list. (MOT already runs to Nov 2026.)
